= Play Style

[quote]
____
Substance over style.
____

EXP leans into rules and tools as opposed to styling.
The typography and layout are intentionally subdued.
The nomenclature is as generic as possible.
The players guide the story and style.

[quote]
____
A humanoid gorilla, a cleaning robot and a tentacled alien run into the jazz bar of a landed spaceship.
On the expedition’s heels are a group of angry mutants driving a stolen yellow bus.
The gorilla fires her lazer rifle at the yellow bus.
The tentacled alien spits poison at the bus tires.
The robot squirts soap on the tarmac of the landing bay.
Before an angry mutant can launch her psionic attack, mental blast, at the gorilla, the yellow bus loses control.
The mutant driver fails to account for soap in the parking lot and crashes the yellow bus.
____

If the above description appeals to you, you will likely enjoy EXP.
The system is for players that want to create their own story.

== EXP is silly
Role-playing should be fun.
EXP is not hard science.
EXP employs pseudo-science, Sciency Fiction and silly science.
The families, vocations and technology are intentionally ironic and are often ridiculous.
EXP wallows in the pink and green light of the absurd.
There will be nuclear-powered toasters, combat robots with welcome mats, and fun-loving aliens.

== EXP is Random
EXP encourages random play.
Players can generate personas randomly.
Personas can acquire mutations randomly.
Players can generate technological artifacts randomly.
Every lookup table includes a die roll column.
This allows players to choose an entry from each table or roll for a random result.
EXP encourages players to “roll with it”; however, choosing from tables is definitely an option.
A lot of players choose to mix and match.
They may choose their vocation but roll their mutations. 

== EXP as Reference
EXP serves more as a reference tool than an art book.
The economics of book production forced publishing houses to break games into supplements, modules and art books.
This parcelling of products complicated play and made games less affordable.
The original goal of EXP was to put everything we needed for play into one document.

.*Reference Principles*
. Create a generic Sciency Fiction role-playing game.
. Create one tome that contained all content.
. Wait decades for this internet thing to catch on.

Initially, EXP was a book.
Books were costly to print.
Technology has advanced, and we no longer have to print a 777-page book for distribution.
You may be consuming this content on a website, a digital PDF or an actual hard copy page.
The living document is available at https://rules.expgame.com[rules.expgame.com].

.*Living Document Aspiration*
. Keep the document in a versioned repository.
. Be free and open source.
. Allow for multiple authors.
. Be downloadable as a book or website.

== EXP is Modular
No one needs to “know” everything in the EXP reference document.
Hence the reference document style. 
The EXP ruleset has many detailed resolution systems.
These resolution systems vary from insidiously complex to refreshingly simple.
EXP works fine employing a fraction of its rule set.
Ideally, EXP modules will help each table build its own Sciency Fiction house rules.

== Conclusion
The nature of role-playing games is very social.
RPGs are for people to get together for a mutual creative experience.
Each player has a persona with a unique appearance and personality.
Groups of players form expeditions of personas that work together in the referee’s milieu.
Together they create stories and laughs.
By design, role-playing games are a social endeavour.

EXP The Game of Technological Chaos is a paper and dice Sciency Fiction role-playing game.
It is *not* a POV shooter video game, movie, or app.
It is *not* a persona record sheet.
It is *not* a collection of die rolls.
It is a game with real people creatively sharing and having fun.
